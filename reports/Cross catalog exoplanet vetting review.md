# Fix EEFN's skewed score, then reposition it

EEFN's cross-catalog idea is worth keeping, but not as the product it is currently sold as. **The headline LightGBM "evidence-consistency" score does not measure cross-catalog evidence.** The served model never sees any catalog disagreement: `HostStar` stores only Gaia values, and `predict.py` copies them into both the "TOI" and "Gaia" slots, so `teff_diff`, `rad_diff` and `dist_diff` are 0 for all 440 candidates with Gaia parameters and missing for the other 106 (repo audit, verified by reading code and the live dev DB). Even when the features are computed correctly (as in training), **all cross-catalog and Gaia features together add only about +0.008 ROC-AUC** over TOI-table columns alone (0.9232 vs 0.9153 under star-grouped CV). Under a temporal split the headline 0.927 AUC falls to about 0.85, and the TOI-only model scores the same. The honest description of the score is "a dispositional prior learned from TOI-table values". This follows from the literature as well: TIC-8 radii are themselves computed from Gaia, so TOI-minus-Gaia differences are mostly method noise, not independent evidence. What the project does have that is defensible is the provenance and audit trail, simple physics and astrometry flags, and an unusually honest self-measurement culture. The change list below is in priority order. Fix the serving bug and republish honest numbers first. Then reposition EEFN as a stellar-input audit and triage-flag tool for TOI reviewers, and put it in front of one TFOP user, which is the one step the project's own board called decisive and the repo shows no sign of having taken.

## The cross-catalog signal is weaker than advertised, for reasons the literature predicts

The ablation numbers are not a quirk of this dataset. The literature gives three independent reasons to expect the cross-catalog features to add little, and the project's own ablation (Model A 0.9137 vs Model E 0.9177, delta +0.0040) agrees.

First, the inputs are not independent. TIC-8 radii come from the Stefan-Boltzmann relation using Gaia DR2 parallax, extinction-corrected G, and a colour-based Teff ([Stassun+2019, arXiv:1905.10694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)), and TOI host parameters come from TIC ([Guerrero+2021, arXiv:2103.12538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538)). A Gaia DR3 FLAME radius shares G, the parallax lineage and the functional form ([Gaia DR3 Apsis II, arXiv:2206.05992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05992)), so differences mostly reflect Teff method, extinction and bolometric-correction differences. The repo bears this out: TIC distance vs 1000/parallax(DR3) differs by a median 0.8%, and `st_rad`/`Rad` has a median ratio of 0.99. One caution on provenance: the repo's own `data_leakage_report.md` labels `st_*` as "pre-TESS archives", which conflicts with the Gaia-derivation claim; the audit treats the TIC-from-Gaia point as inference, though the Stassun text I am citing supports it directly. Also, the Teff colour relation has about 122 K rms scatter, so a radius or Teff threshold below roughly 10-15% or 250-300 K flags mostly noise (inference from the same source).

Second, the mechanisms that make a catalog record wrong are already attacked elsewhere. Blends and nearby eclipsing binaries are handled by TRICERATOPS ([Giacalone+2021](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/1721.1/134566/2/Giacalone_2021_AJ_161_24.pdf)), by centroid and difference-image tests, by TFOP SG1/SG3, and RUWE is already a scalar input to ExoMiner++ ([arXiv:2502.09790](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09790v3)). RUWE alone is also a poor clearance: TOI 864.01 has a single-star Gaia solution with RUWE 1.18 yet a 0.04" companion and a likely hierarchical EB ([arXiv:2601.02171](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02171v4); a single-author preprint, and an earlier version argued the opposite, so treat it as an illustration, not a settled result). Meanwhile, the repo's own RUWE-only AUC is 0.598, and the RUWE > 1.4 flag has precision 0.81 for FP labels (396 triggers, 74 false), which is a good flag but one TFOP already uses.

Third, the label circularity the board worried about is mostly moot in an uncomfortable way. Dropping all Gaia-derived features costs only 0.008 AUC, so Gaia-leakage is not what drives the score. The real circularity sits in the TOI-table columns (SPOC DV depth and radius, which reviewers use to assign dispositions), and the available labels cannot test it. Examples of rule reproduction in the labelled set: `pl_rade` > 30 R_Earth is 0/31 positive, and below 2 R_Earth is 79% positive. The two published "leakage ablations" use weak proxies: pre/post-EDR3 splits re-split independently (test n=306 and 228) using the old Random Forest, and an RV-confirmed "clean holdout" of 104 positives only, which cannot detect false positives and may match sibling TOIs at RV hosts (not verified in code). The literature backs the concern: leakage affects results across fields ([Kapoor & Narayanan, arXiv:2207.07048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.07048)), and the cleaner precedent in the vetting literature restricts labels to confirmed planets plus certified FPs ([ExoMiner, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2111.10009)). A further inference, not a measurement: training only on resolved dispositions (CP/KP/FP/FA = 2,667, 49.6% positive) means the score is not a planet probability for the unresolved PC population it would actually be used on.

On novelty, the literature check found no paper that builds a TIC-vs-Gaia disagreement score and trains it against TFOP dispositions, but the research notes state absence of search hits is weak evidence. The ideas themselves are established: GALAH-based radius revisions made candidates non-planetary ([arXiv:2111.14883](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.14883), via a search summary with unverified author attribution), and SED-based reanalysis cut median radius error from 4.3% to 1.2% ([BAAS abstract](https://baas.aas.org/pub/2022n6i320p04)).

## The stellar-density and centroid pieces need smaller, specific corrections

The centroid redesign is directionally sound. Shift divided by depth is DV's own source-offset relation (Eq. 13-14 of [Twicken+2018, arXiv:1803.04526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.04526)), and replacing white-noise errors with an empirical fake-epoch null is consistent with what DV (whitening) and Bryson+2013 (bootstrap) do ([Bryson+2013, arXiv:1303.0052](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1303.0052)); the TOI-104.01 retraction from 5.4 to 1.97 sigma is exactly the expected direction. Three issues remain. The 2 arcsec floor is a Kepler convention (Kepler pixels were 3.98"), whereas SPOC refuses to call TESS offsets below 7.5" significant at 3 sigma and LEO-Vetter fails sources beyond 15" ([Guerrero+2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538); [Kunimoto+2025, arXiv:2509.10619](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10619v1)). A back-of-envelope estimate in the notes puts a 1000 ppm event's offset precision at ten-plus arcsec, so the 3-sigma cut binds long before the floor does; report the empirical 3-sigma radius and minimum detectable offset per target instead (an estimate, not a measurement). Second, flux-weighted photocentre methods fail when the true source sits outside the aperture, because depth is underestimated and the offset inflated (the KOI-140 case, 19.8" measured vs 5.8" true), so the denominator must be the in-aperture depth and the reference point stated. Third, SPOC already publishes DV offsets per TCE, so EEFN should show them side by side and report disagreements rather than only recomputing. Only 5 of 546 candidates are centroid-measured (all TOI-101 to 105), and none is flagged, with no labelled validation set; a minimal credible study would use TFOP CP/KP controls (100+) against NEB or resolved-companion FPs, pre-specify the threshold, and report false-alarm rate on confirmed planets. Note that the DV-comparison and validation-design items are the research notes' own proposals, since no standard exists. The notes also found no published false-negative rate for any TESS centroid test.

Two smaller flags. The research notes, without reading EEFN code, derived that a stellar-density check that assumes b = 0 should overestimate density, so a reported 24% low result for TRAPPIST-1b more likely reflects a missing (1+k) factor (which would give about 23% low). Check the formula; this is a hypothesis, not a finding. And a derived-radius "unphysical" cut at 22 R_Earth is reasonable, but SPOC already crowding-corrects the PDCSAP depth, so any second dilution correction would double count, and the sqrt(depth) approximation degrades at large k.

## How previous reviews fared, and what the evidence says about adoption

Of the board's five risks, R-2 (Postgres path), R-4 (Arq queue) and R-5 (`pyproject.toml`, `eefn` CLI) are done in code; R-3 (target leakage) is attempted with weak proxies and without disclosing temporal degradation; and R-1, rated CRITICAL (integrate with ExoFOP/MAST, avoid a standalone dashboard), is only partial: exports (VOTable/CSV/JSON), a resolver, webhooks and a CLI exist, but ExoFOP integration is explicitly not done and the product is still primarily a Next.js dashboard. The "highest leverage" E.5 step, one TFOP-affiliated astronomer using it for a season, has no evidence of progress in the repo (outreach outside the repo is invisible to this audit), and the E.4 empirical comparison was never run even in the cheap form `TOOL_COMPARISON.md` describes: correlating EEFN scores with already-published TRICERATOPS FPP and ExoMiner values for overlapping TOIs. The astronomy board's top publication blocker (reframe away from "planet probability") is only partly done: the field is renamed `evidence_consistency_score` and the UI disclaims it, but the target is still planet-vs-FP. Every remaining open item improves internal rigor rather than adoption, which is the pattern to break.

The literature explains why adoption is hard and what has worked. TFOP triage runs on SPOC DV, TESS-ExoClass and human TEV vetting, then SG1-SG5 follow-up coordinated through ExoFOP-TESS ([HEASARC TFOP](https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/docs/tess/tfop.html); [Guerrero+2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538)). I found no documented path by which community tools graduate into TFOP, and no adoption statistics, so the following is inference: small tools such as LATTE ([JOSS, 10.21105/joss.02101](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.02101)) and tpfplotter ([EMAC](https://tools.emac.gsfc.nasa.gov/tpfplotter/)) stuck by doing one task observers already did by hand, installing in one command, being citable, and keying on TIC/TOI IDs, without asking anyone to trust a new classifier. A new numeric score is a bigger ask than a diagnostic plot.

## Worth pursuing, but as a stellar-input audit rather than an evidence score

The core idea, auditing whether a TOI's host-star inputs are trustworthy, is worth pursuing; the packaging as a probability-like score trained on dispositions is not. The notes' assessment is that the tool is partly subsumed (signal-level vetters and imaging already cover the underlying mechanisms) and partly open: no tool offers a lightweight, transparent, light-curve-free audit of a TOI record's internal consistency. The most credible narrative the data support, in the audit's words, is that EEFN reproduces TFOP dispositions at AUC about 0.85-0.92 from TOI-table values, that adding Gaia/2MASS/PS1 barely helps, that physics flags and provenance are the real contribution, and that light-curve and centroid evidence is early and unvalidated.

The repositioning I recommend (my judgement, built on the evidence above) is "catalog hygiene for TOI reviewers": flag TOIs whose stellar inputs make the derived planet radius, priority or interpretation suspect, show the underlying numbers with sources, and never claim to rival DV, TRICERATOPS or ExoMiner++ (ExoMiner++ reports PR AUC about 0.97 on a different, signal-based task; do not compare numerically). Concretely, the product is a one-page triage CSV/VOTable keyed on TOI/TIC ID, with each flag carrying a cited threshold and its provenance. Suitable flags are RUWE in release-keyed tiers (1.25 for "possible binary" under DR3, 1.4 as "likely"; the notes cite [arXiv:2511.02476](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02476v1) and [arXiv:2403.12146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12146)), a Gaia neighbour census within 2-3 TESS pixels with delta-mag and could-it-produce-the-depth logic, derived-radius sanity, sigma-normalized TIC-vs-Gaia outliers, and the density/duration check, with the light-curve and centroid outputs shown beside SPOC DV values. The wording for RUWE must change: "no astrometric evidence of an unresolved companion" is defensible, "no companion" is not, because Gaia misses roughly 45% of companions within 3.5" ([arXiv:2202.01798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01798), via a search summary).

The test of this repositioned product differs from the current one. Do not ask whether the flags predict TFOP FP dispositions better than a TOI-table baseline; they mostly will not. Ask instead whether flagged TOIs later receive revised radii or hosts, or FP dispositions established by follow-up, and whether the flag adds anything beyond radius, period, depth, Tmag and RUWE alone under star-grouped, time-forward evaluation. The project can run this prospective test for free: score the unresolved PCs from the nightly snapshots and compare with eventual dispositions, recording FP provenance (imaging, NEB, RV) where ExoFOP exposes it. Pair it with the empirical E.4 comparison against published TRICERATOPS and ExoMiner values for overlapping TOIs, then put the CSV in front of one SG1 or SG3 member for a season.

One scheduling point: Gaia DR4 is planned for 2 December 2026 with a new reference epoch (J2017.5), independent source IDs mapped through `dr3_neighbourhood`, and a likely RUWE threshold near 1.15 ([ESA DR4 page](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4); [arXiv:2511.02476](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02476v1)). `catalog_registry.py` is only partly abstracted (hardcoded VizieR column names and ingest SQL), so a DR4 swap would break. Do not rush it: store `gaia_release` and source ID per row, keep the pipeline on DR3, and add DR4 later as a separate versioned run. TIC remains DR2-based, and the notes advise not assuming a new TIC.

## Confidence and source quality

High confidence: the repo defects in items 1-3 and 5 (verified in code, and the audit's recomputation matched shipped metrics exactly), the +0.008 and temporal-split findings, and the status of R-1 to R-5 inside the repo. Moderate confidence: the TIC-from-Gaia circularity (supported by primary Stassun and Guerrero text, but the repo's own report disagrees on provenance and the audit labels it inference), the centroid-floor analysis (primary DV and Guerrero text, plus order-of-magnitude arithmetic that has not been run on EEFN data), and the vetter and tool-landscape claims, many of which came via search summaries (flagged below). Low confidence or unverified: whether the production deployment shows the same defects; whether the 104 RV matches are the RV planets or siblings; how much of the vetter's TESS signal comes from catalog inputs; the (1+k) density hypothesis; the stated adoption mechanisms (inference, no data); and the novelty claim, which rests on absence of search hits.

Weak or secondhand sources the notes themselves flag, which I have kept out of load-bearing claims: ExoMiner/ExoMiner++ stellar inputs and several recall figures (search summaries); TRICERATOPS and DAVE use of Gaia (PDF unreadable, unconfirmed); the TOI-FP fraction statistics (could not pin numbers to specific papers; verify before citing); the ExoNet period-distribution shift paper and the 2026 HZ-search review (secondhand); a 2026 Nakamori poster and CMU capstone poster (not peer reviewed); the tabular examples on KOI (a student project); Guo+2017 calibration and Bengio-Grandvalet variance claims (background knowledge, not fetched); and the single-author TOI 864.01 preprint. No source reports zero-shot Kepler-to-TESS accuracy, so I do not quote a benchmark for the drop.

## Conclusion

The sharpest finding is that EEFN's most careful work audited claims, not the serving path. Its honesty effort is real (it retracted the TOI-104.01 centroid result and the CNN), yet the largest defect lives in the one place no test compares training and serving, and the documentation of validation (hardcoded prose, self-issued PASS reports) can say the opposite of what its own tables show. The remedy is cheap and mostly mechanical: one parity test, honest splits, deleted artifacts.

The strategic implication is that the project's value is in what it exposes, not in what it predicts. A reviewer does not need another number that mimics a disposition; they need a fast, sourced list of reasons a TOI's host-star record might be wrong, with the SPOC offset and the Gaia neighbour census beside the project's own. If the flags then show, prospectively, that they anticipate radius revisions or follow-up FPs, the project has an answer to "what does this add to DV and TRICERATOPS". If they do not, the audit trail and exports remain a legitimate small tool, and the project will have learned that cheaply and honestly.
