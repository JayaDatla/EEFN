# Cross-catalog (Gaia / TIC / 2MASS / PS1 / NASA Exoplanet Archive) pitfalls for TESS TOI vetting

Scope note: research done 2026-10-13 with ~15 tool calls. Primary papers were read where the PDF could be text-extracted (Stassun+2019 TIC-8, Gaia DR3 Apsis II, Guerrero+2021 TOI catalog); other items rely on search-result summaries and are flagged. Items I could not source are in Gaps, not Cited Findings.

## 1. TIC-8 vs Gaia DR3 parameters: how derived, offsets, is "TOI minus Gaia" meaningful, is it circular?

### Takeaway
TIC-8 stellar radius is itself computed from Gaia DR2 parallax + Gaia G + a colour-based Teff via Stefan-Boltzmann, and the TOI pipelines take their host parameters from TIC-8. A TOI-minus-Gaia-DR3 difference is therefore not an independent check: it mostly measures DR2-vs-DR3 differences, different Teff/colour relations, different extinction treatment and different bolometric corrections (systematics), and only occasionally a true outlier (giant/binary/bad photometry). It is useful as an outlier flag, not as a physical disagreement metric.

### Cited Findings
- TIC-8 radius uses the Stefan-Boltzmann relation with distance from Gaia parallax via Bailer-Jones+2018 (DR2), extinction-corrected G, Teff (spectroscopic if available, else from dereddened colours), and a Gaia-band bolometric correction (Andrae+2018 MARCS polynomials, 3300-8000 K; own PHOENIX fit 8000-12000 K). No radius for Teff > 12000 K. — [Stassun+2019, arXiv:1905.10694](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- TIC-8 photometric Teff comes from a Gaia G_BP-G_RP colour relation, valid G_BP-G_RP = -0.2 to +3.5, rms scatter 122 K vs spectroscopic Teff (about 70 K intrinsic after removing ~100 K spectroscopic error); less reliable for G_BP-G_RP < 0 and for M stars with G_BP-G_RP > 2. — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- Dereddened-colour Teff vs spectroscopic Teff for ~2 million TICv7 stars: mean difference 20 K, 95th percentile range -410 K to +220 K, skewed to photometric Teff too cool (reddening under-corrected). Schlegel dust maps overestimate E(B-V) > 0.15 by ~1.4x; errors worse within 10 deg of the Galactic plane. — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- For M dwarfs TIC-8 takes parameters from a specially curated Cool Dwarf list rather than the colour relation; stars outside the curated radius/Teff treatment (giants, R > 5 Rsun) get no mass; subgiants get masses "to be regarded with caution". — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- TIC-8 explicitly did NOT adopt curated literature host parameters for known planet hosts, because Exoplanet Archive host parameters are heterogeneous; hosts get "standard procedure" parameters. — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- TIC-8 base catalog is Gaia DR2; v8.1 (Jul 2020) and v8.2 (Sep 2021) followed. Release notes warn that some parameter errors exceed the parameter value, that methods are under development, and that poor catalog photometry affects results; 2MASS sources that Gaia splits get disposition SPLIT with new TIC IDs and `duplicate_id` pointers. — [STScI TIC 8.1/8.2 notes](https://outerspace.stsci.edu/x/bBSeBg)
- TOI catalog: SPOC/QLP used TICv7 parameters for sectors 1-13 and TICv8 afterward; QLP also used Gaia DR2. Moving TICv7 to TICv8 changed some radii enough to turn candidates into too-large-for-a-planet false positives. — [Guerrero+2021, arXiv:2103.12538](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538)
- Gaia DR3 FLAME only runs on sources with GSP-Phot APs (G < 18.25) or GSP-Spec (G < 14); FLAME assumes solar metallicity (use cautiously for [M/H] < -0.5); radii compared to asteroseismic giants agree at 1% with 4% scatter; luminosities vs bolometric-flux literature: median offset 2-3%, dispersion 5-6%. — [Gaia DR3 Apsis II, arXiv:2206.05992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05992)
- GSP-Phot on solar analogs underestimates Teff by 30 K (PHOENIX) to 90 K (MARCS), sigma ~100 K; GSP-Spec bias +10 K, sigma ~130 K. GSP-Phot underestimates logg by 0.12 dex. PHOENIX vs MARCS libraries give "systematic and significant discrepancies" (libname_gspphot matters). — [Apsis II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05992)
- GSP-Phot overestimates Teff for giants and underestimates for supergiants; GSP-Phot assumes single stars, so for unresolved binaries it returns a luminosity-weighted, too-cool Teff/logg. — [Apsis II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05992)
- GSP-Phot 2R/d validated against interferometric angular diameters only for G < 9.6 stars with high-quality parallaxes; FLAME-vs-literature validation likewise is mostly for bright/giant/main-sequence benchmarks. — [Apsis II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05992)
- Cool stars: Gaia DR3 FLAME/GSP-Phot parameters (radius, Teff, A_G) should be used with caution for Teff < 4200 K (systematic overestimation found in PLATO M-dwarf work); radius offsets 10-25% (PHOENIX GSP-Phot), ~7% (MARCS), ~13% (FLAME from GSP-Phot) for R < 0.6 Rsun; FLAME uses GSP-Phot Teff that is ~85 K hotter than ESP-UCD. — [PLATO M-dwarf paper, arXiv:2512.14593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14593) and [Gaia radius paper arXiv:2406.00229](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00229) (figures as summarised in search result; not verified against full text)
- Gaia parallax zero point: EDR3/DR3 quasar median about -17 uas, depends on G, colour (nu_eff) and ecliptic latitude, with different functions for 5- and 6-parameter solutions (Lindegren+2021). Independent tests find residuals for bright (G < 13) blue stars, ~+26 uas offset for red clump, and >10 uas positional variations remain. — [Lindegren+2021 / follow-ups](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08128)
- Stassun & Torres eclipsing-binary test of EDR3 zero point: reported as roughly -37 +/- 20 uas for 5 < G < 12 in later citations (another citation says -15 +/- 18; I could not confirm which is the headline). — [Stassun & Torres 2021 arXiv:2101.03425](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.03425) (via search summary)
- TIC-8 distances are Bailer-Jones+2018 (DR2) distances; the Stassun+2019 text I extracted contains no mention of a parallax zero-point correction in the radius calculation. — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)

### Inferences
- Circularity: TIC-8 R* ~ d x 10^(-0.2(G-A_G)) x Teff^-2 x BC. Gaia DR3 FLAME R* ~ the same ingredients (DR3 parallax, G, Teff from GSP-Phot or GSP-Spec, BC). They share G magnitude, parallax lineage and the same functional form; they differ in Teff (colour relation vs BP/RP-spectrum fit), extinction (dust map vs fitted A_0), and BC. So a "difference" is dominated by Teff and extinction method differences, not independent measurements. Expect typical |dR/R| of order 5-15% for FGK dwarfs from method differences alone, larger for M dwarfs (where TIC uses the cool-dwarf list and Gaia is biased high by 10-25%).
- Because 1 sigma of a ~100 K Teff difference at 5800 K gives ~3.5% in R via T^-2 (and the colour-Teff rms is 122 K), any threshold below roughly 10-15% in radius or ~250-300 K in Teff will flag mostly noise. A threshold should be sigma-normalised using TIC and Gaia reported errors plus a floor for systematics.
- The TOI-vs-Gaia Teff difference has a sign bias: TIC colour Teff tends cool where reddening is underestimated; GSP-Phot is 30-90 K cool for solar analogs and cool for unresolved binaries. Report the median offset by Teff bin and subtract it before flagging.
- Giants/subgiants: if TIC radius and Gaia radius both are large (R > ~2-3 Rsun) but a planet-sized depth gives Rp > 22 Re, that signals a likely evolved host and a legitimate FP flag; but a TIC giant flag should be compared to Gaia logg/FLAME evolstage rather than only radius.
- Stassun's own text says known-host curated parameters were not adopted, so Exoplanet Archive vs TIC differences for confirmed planets reflect heterogeneity of literature values, not TIC error alone.

### Gaps
- Quantitative TIC-8 vs Gaia DR3 FLAME population-level radius/Teff offset statistics (e.g. median and scatter for TOI hosts): not found in a citable source. EEFN can compute this empirically from its own cross-match.
- Paegert+2021 (TIC 8.2) paper text was not retrieved; only the STScI release notes.
- Whether the TOI catalogue columns currently on ExoFOP/Archive still derive from TIC-8 (versus later TOI-specific updates or TIC 8.2) per TOI was not verified.
- Whether FLAME radius flags (`flags_flame`) were considered by the EEFN code was not checked.

## 2. RUWE and other multiplicity indicators; Gaia neighbours vs TIC/CROWDSAP

### Takeaway
RUWE > 1.4 is a DR2-era convention; a release-specific threshold for DR3 is closer to 1.25 and for DR4 about 1.15. RUWE is sensitive to photocentre motion on orbital periods from roughly months to a decade or more with projected separations of tens of mas, so it misses the close, short-period and wide companions most relevant to TESS blends (e.g. a 0.04 arcsec hierarchical EB with RUWE 1.18). It should be one flag among several, never a clearance.

### Cited Findings
- RUWE is defined in Gaia technical note GAIA-C3-TN-LU-LL-124 (Lindegren) and was normalised to be ~1 for typical well-behaved sources; "RUWE below 1.40" is the documented suggested cut. — [Gaia archive documentation (search summary)](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Catalogue_consolidation/chap_cu9gat/sec_cu9gat_catplots/sec_cu9gat_othersastro.html)
- Penoyre et al. 2022a recommended RUWE > 1.25 as appropriate for DR3; many papers still use 1.4. — [Gaia binary review, arXiv:2403.12146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12146)
- Release-specific thresholds: DR2 1.37, DR3 1.25, DR4 1.15, DR5 1.11. Detectability peaks near each release baseline; long-period detectable fraction rises ~10-20% per release, short-period ~5-10%; low-to-moderate eccentricity detectable to ~100 yr from DR4 onward; using the fixed DR2 value of 1.4 causes significant loss of short-period binaries. The paper restricts angular separations to < 180 mas and does not analyse separation dependence. — [Nominal thresholds for good astrometric fits, arXiv:2511.02476](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02476v1)
- RUWE signal amplitude scales approximately linearly with photocentre orbit angular size; sensitivity cited as periods of months to ~a decade; a Be-star sample found sensitivity to 0.04-0.7 arcsec separations (sample-specific). Gaia sources with G < ~4 / saturated are unreliable. — [Gaia binary review, arXiv:2403.12146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12146)
- Elevated RUWE in dense sky regions for G ~ 11-12.5 creates an artificial dearth of sources at a 1.4 cut, cause not understood. — [Gaia documentation summary](https://gea.esac.esa.int/archive/documentation/GDR3/Catalogue_consolidation/chap_cu9gat/sec_cu9gat_catplots/sec_cu9gat_othersastro.html)
- Ziegler et al. SOAR TESS Survey I: high or null RUWE infers a companion with ~86% accuracy in that survey; Gaia does not resolve rho < ~0.7-0.8 arcsec. — [SOAR TESS survey papers](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26250) (via search summary of Ziegler+2020)
- A companion-census study found ~45% of companions within 3.5 arcsec not detected by Gaia, and noted TOI hosts with known companions have RUWE inflated by the companion; 238 high-confidence companions to 234 of 2140 TOI hosts (~10.9% raw rate). — [Stellar companions to TOIs, arXiv:2202.01798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01798) (via search summary)
- TOI 864.01: Gaia DR3 single-star solution with RUWE = 1.18, but TFOP SG1 imaging found a companion at 0.04 arcsec; signal interpreted as a hierarchical EB. — [arXiv:2601.02171](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.02171v4)
- Other DR3 binarity columns: `ipd_frac_multi_peak` (resolved doubles; sensitive to somewhat larger separations than RUWE), `ipd_gof_harmonic_amplitude` (intended for resolved doubles but reported in DR3 to reflect photometric variability due to a processing error, so not an unresolved-binarity indicator), `astrometric_excess_noise(_sig)`, `duplicated_source` (some well-studied stars carry wrong flags), `non_single_star`. A VVV study combined RUWE > 2, IPD amp > 0.1 with RUWE > 1.4, multi-peak fraction > 30, duplicated_source = 1, non_single_star != 0. — [arXiv:2607.24510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.24510) (via search summary)
- TESS pixel is 21 arcsec; SPOC CROWDSAP is built from TIC magnitudes and proper-motion-corrected TIC coordinates plus a PRF; correction applied to PDCSAP only; photometry less reliable for CROWDSAP < ~0.8. TIC contamination ratio searches within 10 TESS pixels using a pre-launch-PSF-based model; it does not model bleed trails. — [Guerrero+2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538), [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- No formal published head-to-head comparison of CROWDSAP vs TIC contamination ratio vs Gaia-neighbour contamination was found; one tool paper (TESSExtractor) notes Gaia-based metrics ignore the PRF and may differ from CROWDSAP/TIC values in crowded fields. A 2025 AAS abstract argues CROWDSAP misestimates bias depths. — [TESSExtractor arXiv:2608.25113](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25113), [BAAS 2025](https://baas.aas.org/pub/2025n4i416p02)

### Inferences
- RUWE <= 1.4 should never be reported as "no companion". Recommended wording: "no astrometric evidence of an unresolved companion with P ~ 0.3-10+ yr and photocentre wobble > ~0.1 mas; silent for sub-0.1 arcsec / short-period (< ~months) / very wide pairs". EEFN's 1.4 is acceptable but conservative-for-purity; use 1.25 (DR3) as a "possible binary" tier and 1.4 as "likely"; record the DR4 value (1.15) in config keyed by release.
- High RUWE is least informative for bright stars (G < ~5 saturation effects) and for stars already known to have wide Gaia-resolved companions (RUWE is inflated by the neighbour's flux at small separations).
- A better TESS-relevant companion test is a Gaia neighbour query within ~2-3 pixels (42-63 arcsec) with delta-mag, converted to expected dilution and to "could this neighbour produce the depth if it were an EB" (the TRICERATOPS/nearby-FP logic), rather than RUWE alone. Combine `ipd_frac_multi_peak`, `duplicated_source` and `non_single_star` as supplementary flags; do not use `ipd_gof_harmonic_amplitude` for binarity in DR3.
- Since CROWDSAP uses TIC (DR2-based) positions with proper-motion correction, comparing it with a fresh DR3 neighbour census is a legitimate consistency check (difference should be small except for new DR3 sources fainter than TIC limits or blends).

### Gaps
- Official TFOP guidance text on how RUWE and Gaia neighbours are used (the ExoFOP guidance URL returned HTTP 404). Only second-hand evidence (SOAR/speckle papers) was obtained.
- Penoyre+2022 and Belokurov+2020 primary texts were not fetched; their findings are known here only via the 2403.12146 review and search summaries.
- Quantitative RUWE sensitivity vs projected separation (mas) and period for TESS-host-like masses/distances; Stassun & Torres on RUWE specifically (they were located for parallax only).

## 3. Cross-match mechanics (radius, epochs, high PM, crowding, 2MASS/PS1, colours)

### Takeaway
A 5 arcsec radius with PM propagation to epoch 2016 is workable for isolated TOIs but, with a nearest-neighbour or first-match policy, can pick the wrong star in crowded fields (TESS targets sit in 21 arcsec pixels, many TOI hosts have Gaia neighbours within 5 arcsec). Matching on TIC ID (or TIC-to-Gaia DR2 ID mapping, then DR2-to-DR3 neighbourhood) is more robust than positional matching. PS1 is saturated at the bright end where many TOI hosts live, and colours add little independent information beyond Gaia Teff except for flagging reddening/photometry problems.

### Cited Findings
- TIC-8 is Gaia DR2 based and carries Gaia DR2 source IDs; 2MASS sources split by Gaia carry SPLIT disposition and `duplicate_id` pointers. — [STScI TIC release notes](https://outerspace.stsci.edu/x/bBSeBg)
- SPOC crowding uses proper-motion-corrected TIC coordinates (so SPOC itself handles epoch propagation to observation time). — [Guerrero+2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538)
- PS1 g, r saturate near 13.5 mag (z ~13.0, y ~12.0); STScI FAQ gives conservative reliable-photometry bright limits g 14.5, r 15, i 15; bright saturated stars get spurious detections/classifications. — [Pan-STARRS1 surveys, arXiv:1612.05560](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05560); [STScI PS1 FAQ](https://outerspace.stsci.edu/x/6Iac)
- PS1 3pi covers Dec > -30 deg with a ragged edge between -31.5 and -30. — [Pan-STARRS1 surveys](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.05560)
- TIC-8 deredden Gaia colours using Pan-STARRS or Schlegel maps: E(G_BP-G_RP) = 1.31 E(B-V), A_G = 2.72 E(B-V), A_T = 2.06 E(B-V) for a solar-like SED; R_G varies with Teff, which biases the Teff-colour relation; maximum permitted E(B-V) 2.5. — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- Gaia DR4 reference epoch will be J2017.5 (DR3: J2016.0). — [ESA DR4 page](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4)

### Inferences
- Radius: TESS aperture is ~1-3 pixels (21-63 arcsec) but the host identification is by TIC ID, so 5 arcsec (≈ 0.24 pixel) is sensible for centroid agreement; but for high-PM stars, the epoch matters: a star with 500 mas/yr moves ~6.5 arcsec between 2000 and 2016. PM propagation to 2016 removes this only if the query catalogue PM is for that epoch; if EEFN's TOI coordinates are epoch 2000 (TIC), propagate TIC PM (also Gaia DR2 PM, epoch 2015.5) to 2016.0 before matching; otherwise the 5 arcsec radius will miss stars with PM > ~300 mas/yr. Recommend: take the closest match AND require magnitude consistency (G vs TIC Tmag-derived or Gaia mag in TIC), report the number of Gaia sources within 5 arcsec, and flag ambiguous matches (second source within 2x separation or delta-G < ~2).
- Better: use the Gaia DR2 source ID stored in TIC (`GAIA` column) as the primary key and map to DR3 via `gaiadr3.dr2_neighbourhood` (and later DR3->DR4 via `dr3_neighbourhood`); positional matching only as fallback. (Inference from TIC and DR4 documentation; the DR2->DR3 table existence is from my background knowledge, not cited here.)
- 2MASS: epoch ~1997-2001, J/H/K saturated for J < ~5-6 (my background knowledge, not cited); 2MASS beam (~2-3 arcsec) blends close pairs; XMatch with 5 arcsec in crowded fields can assign the combined flux of a blend to the primary. Both PS1 (Dec > -30 and saturation near 13.5) and 2MASS lose bright TOI hosts, so missing colours are often "bright star" rather than "bad data": treat as not-applicable rather than as a completeness penalty.
- Colours: with Gaia G_BP-G_RP (already in TIC Teff) available, J-K and g-r give only a weakly independent Teff estimate; their value is as a reddening/photometric-consistency check (e.g. Gaia-Teff vs J-K Teff disagreeing by > ~200-300 K suggests blended photometry, giant/dwarf confusion, or underestimated extinction), and a way to catch a bright unresolved companion (colour excess). Extinction affects J-K much less than g-r (A_J-K ≈ 0.17 A_V vs E(g-r) ≈ 0.7 A_V; my background knowledge, not cited), so use J-K, dereddened with the same map, and do not compare g-r thresholds across sky regions without dereddening. Do not use a PS1-derived disagreement at Dec < -30 or G < 13.5.

### Gaps
- Cited false-match rates for 5 arcsec CDS XMatch on TESS TOIs, and statistics for how many TOI hosts have ≥ 2 Gaia sources within 5 arcsec: not found; can be computed from EEFN's own output.
- 2MASS saturation limits and PS1-vs-Gaia colour-Teff transformations were not sourced here.

## 4. Stellar-density test (Seager & Mallen-Ornelas 2003), b = 0 bias, TRICERATOPS

### Takeaway
Transit-derived stellar density is degenerate with impact parameter, eccentricity and limb darkening. Assuming b = 0 maximises duration for a given a/R*, so the inferred density is a lower limit for a circular orbit with unknown b (which matches the 24% low result on TRAPPIST-1b only if the true b is not ~0 and the star/planet params are accurate). A density mismatch is a weak FP discriminator on its own; the community uses it as a consistency prior inside full fits and with Bayesian FPP tools.

### Cited Findings
- Seager & Mallen-Ornelas 2003: a unique solution for planet/star parameters from a transit light curve exists only for a circular orbit with two or more transits and negligible limb darkening; stellar density can then be derived from the light curve alone. — [Seager & Mallen-Ornelas 2003](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/astro-ph/0210076)
- Derived stellar density is strongly correlated with b, Rp/R* and duration (a larger lower-density star with higher b vs smaller denser star with lower b); worst for grazing geometries. — [Asterodensity / density-impact parameter discussion, arXiv:2609.16201](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.16201) (via search summary)
- Photo-eccentric effect: rho* = g(e,w)^-3 rho_circ, so density uncertainty determines eccentricity sensitivity; ~10% σ_g is plausible for high-SNR spectroscopically characterised hosts, much worse at low SNR; a loose density prior can still constrain e because a/R* ∝ rho^(1/3). Mismatch can also come from photo-blend, photo-spot, photo-timing, photo-duration, photo-mass effects (Kipping 2014). — [Kipping 2014, arXiv:1311.1170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.1170); [Dawson & Johnson 2012, arXiv:1203.5537](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.5537); [Kipping 2014b arXiv:1412.0014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.0014)
- TRICERATOPS (Giacalone+2021) computes Bayesian FPP and NFPP over scenarios (transiting planet on target, bound/chance-aligned companions, nearby stars, EBs), with validation thresholds FPP < 0.015 and NFPP < 1e-3; requires a flattened light curve. I found no source showing it performs an explicit stellar-density consistency test. — [TRICERATOPS publication page](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/publication/2025AJ....170..148G) and [vetting 384 TOIs](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q6334/vetting-of-384-tess-objects-of-interest-with-triceratops-and-statistical-validation-of-12-planet-candidates)

### Inferences
- Direction of the b = 0 bias (my derivation, not cited): for fixed T14, a/R* ~ (P/(pi T14)) sqrt((1+k)^2 - b^2), and rho* ~ (a/R*)^3. Assuming b = 0 therefore OVERESTIMATES a/R* and rho* by [(1+k)^2/((1+k)^2 - b^2)]^(3/2) when the true b > 0. So b = 0 cannot explain a density that is 24% LOW. A likely cause: dropping the (1+k) factor (using a/R* ~ P/(pi T) instead of P/(pi T)*(1+k)), which for TRAPPIST-1b (k ~ 0.09) gives (1.09)^-3 = 0.77, i.e. ~23% low, matching the observed ~24%. Recommend EEFN check the formula for a missing (1+k) term (and whether T is T14 or T23/ingress-inclusive). Not verified against EEFN code.
- Test power: with sigma_rho from isochrone/TIC of ~20-30% (R ~ 7-10%, M ~ 15-20%, so rho ~ 25-35%) and ~10-30% from the light curve for modest-SNR TOIs, only mismatches > ~factor 1.5-2 are informative; circular-orbit planets at P < ~10 d are the best case (circularised). It will flag giants/evolved hosts and some blends but cannot separate many EBs, and it will falsely flag legitimately eccentric planets and high-b transits.
- Recommended design: compare observed transit duration to the maximum expected duration for that density, i.e. flag only when T_obs is longer than the b = 0, circular upper limit (and shorter only with a b prior), propagate sigma, report "duration/expected" with uncertainty, and mark it informational. A proper alternative is fitting rho* as a prior in a transit model (juliet/exoplanet style) with a free b and e, or using TRICERATOPS/ vespa for FP probability.

### Gaps
- Quantitative FP-discrimination performance (ROC/recall) of a stellar-density test on TOIs: no source found.
- I did not read EEFN code, so the (1+k) hypothesis for the 24% low TRAPPIST-1b result is unverified.

## 5. Gaia DR4 (planned 2 December 2026): what changes for a DR3-hardcoded pipeline

### Takeaway
DR4 is a major schema and identifier change. DR4 source IDs must be treated as independent of DR3 and mapped through `dr3_neighbourhood`; reference epoch changes to J2017.5; RUWE thresholds shift (1.15); `gaia_source` uses best-fit model per source; astrophysical-parameter and non-single-star content is reorganised. A DR3-hardcoded ingestion should be isolated behind a release-versioned adapter and run side-by-side, not swapped in place.

### Cited Findings
- DR4 is planned for 2 December 2026, based on 66 months of data (DR3: 34), ~2.8 billion sources overall with ~2 billion high-quality in `gaia_source`, ~400-500 TB (DR3: 10 TB), 140+ tables, 60+ papers; ESA says content is decided after validation, so figures may shift. A 29 June 2026 astrometric time-series sample was released for preparation. — [ESA Gaia news 2026](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/news-2026), [ESA DR4 page](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4)
- Reference epoch J2017.5 (DR3 J2016.0). New `epoch_astrometry` table (~129.6 billion records, DataLink); four per-pipeline tables (astrometry, photometry, RVS, flags) complement `gaia_source`; NSS tables include `nss_two_body_orbit`, `nss_acceleration_astro`, `nss_resolved_pair`, `nss_multiple_orbits`, `nss_vim_fl`, `nss_masses`; `gaia_source` uses the best-suited model per source (e.g. binary instead of the single-star default). — [ESA DR4 page](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4)
- The DR4 source list "should be treated as independent" of DR3/DR2/DR1; identifiers may change or disappear; `dr3_neighbourhood` exists because equal IDs do not guarantee the same star; a DR3 source may have zero, one or several DR4 neighbours. Parameters in `gaia_source` are consolidated from several modules so may differ from DR3 defaults. Draft data model dated 2026-06-26; a known-issues list will be published. — [ESA DR4 page](https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/gaia/dr4)
- RUWE: the ESA DR4 page does not mention RUWE; a theoretical paper predicts the appropriate DR4 threshold ≈ 1.15. — [arXiv:2511.02476](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02476v1)

### Inferences
- Mitigations: (1) store `gaia_release` and `gaia_source_id` per row and never join across releases on ID; (2) use `dr3_neighbourhood` (or position+PM+G matching) with explicit handling of 0/1/many matches and persist match quality; (3) propagate positions with the release epoch (2016.0 vs 2017.5) in code via config, not constants; (4) feature-detect columns (schema introspection) and keep a column-mapping layer, treating RUWE availability as "unknown" until the DR4 data model is final; (5) treat DR3 vs DR4 differences for the same star as a data-quality signal (large parallax/PM change or a new NSS orbit) rather than silently overwriting; (6) rerun threshold calibration (RUWE 1.25 -> 1.15) and zero-point/ FLAME validity checks since DR4 zero-point and astrophysical parameters are not yet published; (7) DR4 time-series astrometry and NSS orbital solutions could make a RUWE-alone binary flag obsolete, so design the flags table around "evidence type" (RUWE, NSS orbit, acceleration, resolved pair).
- Timing risk: the release is ~7 weeks after the date of this research; sticking to DR3 for EEFN's current product and adding DR4 as a separate, versioned later run is the safer plan. TIC remains DR2-based unless STScI releases a new TIC; do not assume a TIC-9.

### Gaps
- Whether DR4 includes RUWE, FLAME, GSP-Phot in the same column names: not stated on the ESA page; check the published data model after release.
- Whether DR4 provides a DR2/TIC mapping table: not stated. Parallax zero-point prescription for DR4: not yet available.

## 6. Does the "Gaia-derived planet radius vs catalog radius" check duplicate the TOI pipeline?

### Takeaway
Largely yes: the TOI/TIC radius is already Gaia-based (via DR2), and TOI planet radii use TIC R* with the SPOC/QLP depth (and SPOC applies crowding/CROWDSAP correction to PDCSAP). The check adds value only when Gaia DR3 FLAME differs materially from TIC or the host has a companion/dilution not modelled, and it should include dilution correctly.

### Cited Findings
- TOI pipelines took stellar parameters from TICv8 (sectors 14+), and a TICv7 to TICv8 radius change turned some candidates into too-large-radius false positives. — [Guerrero+2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538)
- SPOC crowding correction applies to the PDCSAP flux (not SAP); CROWDSAP reports the target-flux fraction; lower values (< ~0.8) mean less reliable photometry; TIC contamination is computed within 10 pixels using a PRF model. — [Guerrero+2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12538), [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- Gaia companions within 3.5 arcsec are missed ~45% of the time, and unresolved companions bias Gaia Teff/logg (luminosity-weighted) as GSP-Phot assumes single stars. — [arXiv:2202.01798](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01798) (search summary); [Apsis II](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05992)

### Inferences
- Rp = R* sqrt(depth) 109.2 is correct to first order; the cutoff of 22 Re (~ 2 RJ... 22 Re ≈ 1.96 RJ) is a sensible "unphysical for a planet" bound for old planets (radius inflation tops out near ~2 RJ for hot Jupiters) but not at high k: a 22 Re value at any depth should be treated as "probably stellar or evolved host", and for k > ~0.2 the sqrt(depth) approximation underestimates k by limb darkening and b (need k from the fit, not sqrt(depth)). Depth in the TOI catalogue is SPOC/QLP PDCSAP depth (already crowding-corrected by SPOC; applying a second dilution correction would double count).
- Because the radius estimates share the Gaia lineage and depth, an agreement carries little new information; disagreement is informative only for (a) hosts where TIC radius is off by > ~15-20% due to bad photometry/reddening/binary, (b) cases where Gaia neighbour dilution exceeds the TIC-modelled contamination, (c) evolved hosts. Use it as "stellar radius sanity check" with a documented caveat and show both Rp values, not a single boolean.
- About 20% of hosts without a Gaia FLAME radius is consistent with FLAME's G < 18.25 (GSP-Phot) and requirement of sufficient precision, and with FLAME not being produced for many sources lacking BP/RP spectra or with poor APs; recommend falling back to TIC-8 radius or a Stefan-Boltzmann/BC estimate from Gaia DR3 parallax + G + A_G, clearly marked as derived.

### Gaps
- Direct evidence of how many TOIs have a TIC-vs-DR3-FLAME radius disagreement > 20%: not found.
- Whether QLP/SPOC TOI planet radii are recomputed with updated TIC (8.2) per release: not verified.

## Cross-cutting notes on "Completeness" and the NASA Exoplanet Archive
- The Stassun+2019 text states that Exoplanet Archive host parameters are heterogeneous and were deliberately not merged into TIC; therefore a "fraction of core fields populated" metric conflates catalog coverage (Dec, brightness, saturation) with data quality. Missing PS1 for Dec < -30 and for bright stars, and missing 2MASS/PS1 for blends, are coverage facts, not quality failures (Inference). — [Stassun+2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10694)
- Not researched: NASA Exoplanet Archive composite-parameter table behavior (default parameter set selection, per-reference inconsistencies) beyond the TIC statement above.
